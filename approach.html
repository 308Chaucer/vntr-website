Approach

How we do things differently

We run sell-side mandates as a staggered process: a stack ranked buyer list, released in waves, stopped once three or four letters of intent are in hand. The method is set out in Funds Flow.

A conventional auction contacts every plausible buyer at once and lets the field sort itself. That places the confidential memorandum with dozens of parties, several of them competitors, and fixes bid dates before the seller knows which buyers are serious.

We work the same screened universe in sequence, starting with the buyers most likely to close, and we stop releasing names as soon as there are enough letters to price the company and hold the selected buyer to its terms.

Process trade-offs

The two processes compared on four dimensions a seller carries in addition to headline price.

DimensionBroad auctionStaggered process
Information exposure28 of 40 parties receive the memorandum, including direct competitors that sign a non-disclosure agreement to read it.9 parties receive the memorandum, released in waves. 22 names on the approved list are never contacted.
Control of paceThe process calendar sets the pace. Bid dates are fixed before the seller knows which buyers are serious.The seller approves the list name by name. A further wave goes out only if the previous one did not produce a letter.
Price formationTension across a wide field, offset by buyers that read a crowded process as a value opportunity and discount for it.Tension among the highest probability buyers first. Value buyers sit at the bottom of the list and are reached last, if at all.
Outcome if no saleThe whole field knows the process failed, and a second process starts from a weaker position.Few parties are aware of the process, so the company can return to market later without that history.

Terms

Engagement and document terms

Net proceeds are decided in the engagement letter and the purchase agreement as well as in the headline price. These are the positions we hold on a sell-side mandate.

Tail

A tail of 3 months on sell-side mandates. A longer tail is reasonable on a buy-side mandate.

Renewal

No automatic renewal provisions. An engagement continues only by agreement of both parties.

Retainer

Retainers are credited in full against the success fee.

Warranty insurance

The buyer pays the representations and warranties premium. The seller does not split it.

Practice

Work we decline

A quality of earnings report sold by the bankerThe tally of EBITDA adjustments is kept by the chief financial officer.
Growth forecasts a buyer will not acceptProjections in the memorandum are ones management can defend in diligence.
A mandate priced above what a process can deliverIf the valuation a seller needs is not supportable, we say so before engagement.
Two clients in one laneBuy-side exclusivity and capital markets exclusivity are both defined in the engagement letter.

From a mandate

On one sell-side mandate the letter of intent that closed came from the seventh name on the list, reached when the process was down to prospect 11.
